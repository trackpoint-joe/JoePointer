AI Delivery Readiness Review

Before anyone trusts an AI solution enough to act on what it produces, the solution should clear five checks. This page walks you through them and tells you whether to accept it or send it back.

An AI solution is a model and the way its output reaches the people who act on it. That output might be a forecast, a risk score, a recommended action, or an answer from an AI assistant.

  1. 1Think of one AI solution your team is being asked to trust.
  2. 2Mark each of the five gates Pass, Gap, or Unknown.
  3. 3Get a verdict and the questions to send back to the team.
Accept
Every check has evidence behind it.
Accept with conditions
It can be acted on once one or two missing answers come back.
Send back
Do not act on it yet. The questions go back to the team.

Accept is the reviewer's recommendation. The owner of the solution still decides.

About two minutes. What you mark and write stays in this browser.

The Five Gates

Mark each gate based on what you actually know about the solution. The verdict updates as you go, in the bar at the bottom of your screen and in the full panel below the gates. Use the pencil beside any question to mark it answered or falling short, or to add a note.

Each gate also shows where the check happens. Some run automatically in the pipeline on every change. Some are a human review. Some are watched after launch.

Human review

Audience

Objectives
  • What outcome should change for them, and who is accountable for it today?
  • What quantity would move if this works?
3
Problem
  • What breaks today that this solution is meant to fix?
  • What will someone do differently because of what it produces?
  • Could a report, a list, or a spreadsheet solve the same problem without AI?

2

Success criteria and baseline

How will you know it is better than the way the work gets done today?
Human review
  • What does success look like, in a number anyone can check?
  • What is the baseline, the simple rule or the current process, that this has to beat?
  • Has anyone actually run that baseline, or is it assumed?
  • What does a false alarm cost compared with a missed problem?
  • Was the threshold chosen with that in mind?
  • Over what population and what period?
Status for Gate 2, Success criteria and baseline
3

Evaluation

Was it tested on data it had never seen, the way it will be used for real?
Human review
  • Was the test data kept separate in time from the data the model learned on?In plain terms: was it graded on a stretch of time it had already learned from?
  • Could any input leak the answer the model is supposed to produce?In plain terms: is there a clue hidden in the data that gives away the answer?
  • Is the test set representative of what the model will see once it is live?In plain terms: does the practice test look like the real thing it will face later?
  • Is the score a calibrated probability, or only a ranking?In plain terms: when it says seventy percent, do about seventy out of a hundred of those turn out that way?
  • Were related records (the same site, the same equipment, the same customer) kept on one side of the split?In plain terms: if ten readings came from one machine, did all ten stay together on one side, training or test, instead of leaking across both?
  • What counts as a failure, and who defined it?In plain terms: someone decided what counts as wrong here. Who made that call, and would you agree with it?
Status for Gate 3, Evaluation
4

Claims check

Can every number reported about this solution be traced to its source?
Automated check where possibleHuman review
  • Does every reported result trace to a named person, a shipped feature, or a linked measurement?
  • Could someone ask "show me" and get an answer in under a minute?
  • Is there a claim here that nobody currently on the team can stand behind?
  • Has anyone checked the number independently, beyond naming where it came from?
Status for Gate 4, Claims check
5

Adoption and value

Are people actually using this solution, and which person owns it after launch?
Monitored after launchHuman review
  • Is this actually used in the work, or only available to use?
  • How is the benefit measured, and who checks it?
  • Who owns this after the team that built it moves on?
  • Do the people using the output trust it, and can they tell you when it is wrong?
  • What is watched after launch, and what triggers a rollback?
  • Is the benefit measured in the same quantity named in Gate 1?
Status for Gate 5, Adoption and value
Send back
Gate 1 (audience, objectives, problem) and Gate 3 (evaluation) have not passed. Both must pass, so this goes back to the team.
How the verdict works. Any gate not marked Pass counts against the solution. Gate 1 and Gate 3 must pass. If either has not, the solution goes back to the team, no matter how the other gates look. A claim marked Gap in Gate 4 sends it back too. Otherwise, all five passed is accept, one or two not passed is accept with conditions, and three or more is send back.

How does my own work score?

The AI I was using on Future Me, Answered invented two testimonials, and they stayed live for three months before a review agent I built caught them.

My rule against invented quotes lived in a comment in one code file. Until that sweep, the review agent had never been pointed at the marketing pages, and it was not on the pull request path.

Here is how the five gates read against it.

  • Gate 1Audience, objectives, problemPassIt was clear who the page was for and what it had to do.
  • Gate 2Success criteria and baselineNot applicableMarketing copy had no success number or baseline at stake.
  • Gate 3EvaluationNot applicableNothing was trained, so there was no model to test.
  • Gate 4Claims checkGapTwo quotes from people who did not exist.
  • Gate 5Adoption and valueGapThe live pages were not being checked after they shipped.
  • VerdictSend backGate 4 alone is enough.

The fix. I moved the rule into the instruction file every AI session loads. Every public claim now has to trace to a named person, a shipped feature, or a linked measurement. My co-founder, Mike Pointer, and I put review agents on every pull request, including the marketing pages.

It came back. Three weeks later the same kind of defect appeared on a different page. This time it was caught on the first commit, before it reached a live page.
